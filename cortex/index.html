Where these deals stall, and what I do about it

"We'll build it on Backstage"

I price the engineers who maintain it, then show Scorecards and Initiatives working on their own catalog in days.

The portal becomes a directory

I tie the deal to one outcome, like peak readiness or a migration, so value shows up in a quarter.

Developers push back

I bring developer experience leads in early and show the golden paths that save them time.

Security review of integrations

I start the security package in discovery, with read only scopes mapped for every integration.

No one owns engineering operations

I find the leader who runs the OpEx review and make them the champion.

Budget sits with platform, value sits with leadership

The business case shows value for both, and the close is timed to the planning cycle.

MEDDPICC checklist

Updated after every call and reviewed with my manager each week. Each line needs written proof before the deal counts in my forecast.

M

Metrics

The number the deal is sold on
What I validate

Incident count, MTTR, deploy frequency, hours spent preparing OpEx reviews, portal upkeep cost, and the cost of an outage in peak season.

What I ask
  • What did your last major incident cost?
  • How many hours go into preparing each operational review?
Proof I collect

Baseline numbers from their own data, checked by engineering leadership and finance, in the business case.

Red flag, and my move

A problem nobody has priced. I bring the H&R Block and Forrester numbers and ask them to correct them for their org.

E

Economic buyer

The person who can say yes alone
What I validate

The CTO or SVP of Engineering, with the CISO when Scorecards cover security, and how they made their last platform decision.

What I ask
  • Who answers to the board when AI speed causes an outage?
  • Whose budget pays for the portal today?
Proof I collect

A meeting with the economic buyer before the evaluation, and their success criteria in their own words.

Red flag, and my move

"I'll take it to the CTO." I write the champion a one page brief and ask for 20 minutes together to pressure test it.

D

Decision criteria

How they will judge us
What I validate

Time to value, catalog accuracy, Scorecards and Initiatives, integrations, AI governance, security and total cost against a Backstage build.

What I ask
  • What has to be true for Cortex to be the clear choice?
  • What would rule us out?
Proof I collect

Written evaluation criteria the champion and I drafted together, before the first integration connects.

Red flag, and my move

Criteria copied from a portal feature list. I reshape the criteria early around outcomes: incidents, MTTR and review time.

D

Decision process

Every step to a signature
What I validate

Evaluation on real services, security review, business case, legal and procurement, with dates, and how they bought their last engineering platform.

What I ask
  • Walk me through how you chose your last engineering platform.
  • Who could stop this, even if everyone else says yes?
Proof I collect

A mutual action plan dated back from the planning cycle or peak season, edited by both sides.

Red flag, and my move

No one can describe what happens after the evaluation. I map the post evaluation steps with the champion before the first Scorecard runs.

P

Paper process

How the contract actually gets signed
What I validate

Security questionnaire, integration scopes, DPA, legal redlines, procurement thresholds and cloud marketplace options.

What I ask
  • What did security need from your last developer tool vendor?
  • Would buying through your cloud marketplace help?
Proof I collect

Security package sent in week one, paper steps and owners in the plan, and a signature date agreed.

Red flag, and my move

Procurement first hears about us at quarter end. I meet procurement as soon as the business case is agreed.

I

Identify pain

Why they act this year
What I validate

An outage in peak season, an AI rollout outrunning governance, a failed audit, a stalled Backstage project or a painful OpEx review.

What I ask
  • What happens if nothing changes for the next 12 months?
  • What broke last quarter that a standard would have caught?
Proof I collect

Pain tied to a dollar figure and a date, confirmed by engineering leadership and the platform team.

Red flag, and my move

Pain with no cost and no deadline. I go back to the rational drowning step with their own incidents until it has both.

C

Champion

Who sells when I am not in the room
What I validate

A platform engineering, SRE or developer experience leader with influence who wins personally if this succeeds. I test it by asking them to act.

What I ask
  • How would you present the evaluation results to your CTO?
  • Could you get us 30 minutes with the security team this week?
Proof I collect

They built a Scorecard with us, coached me on the room, and got me a meeting I could not get alone.

Red flag, and my move

A fan of the product with no pull on the decision. I keep them close and multithread to someone who can move the deal.

C

Competition

Every option they could pick
What I validate

Backstage, Port, OpsLevel, Atlassian Compass and DX, spreadsheets and doing nothing, plus who inside favors each.

What I ask
  • If you don't choose us, what do you do instead?
  • Who on the team would push for that option?
Proof I collect

Each alternative named with its internal sponsor, and our strengths built into the evaluation criteria.

Red flag, and my move

We learn about the competitor at the proposal. I ask the battlecard questions in discovery, so the criteria reflect where Cortex wins.

Commit ruleA deal enters commit only when the economic buyer has confirmed the metrics, the evaluation has met written criteria, the paper process and partner steps are dated in the mutual plan, and the champion has been tested.
