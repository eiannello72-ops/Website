How deals are won against each alternative

Where each option is strong, where Cortex wins, and the question I would ask in discovery. Select an alternative to open its battlecard.

Backstage

Most common

Where they are strong

Free to start, huge plugin ecosystem, and full control for a strong platform team.

Where Cortex wins

Live in days, with Scorecards, Initiatives and the OpEx Review Agent built in, and no team assigned to maintain the portal.

The question I ask

How many engineers keep your Backstage running, and what would they build instead?

Port

High threat

Where they are strong

Fast to start, a generous free tier and a flexible data model.

Where Cortex wins

Where these deals stall

Six stalls I plan for from the first meeting, and the MEDDPICC checklist every deal runs against.

"We'll build it on Backstage"

I price the engineers who maintain it, then show Scorecards and Initiatives working on their own catalog in days.

The portal becomes a directory

I tie the deal to one outcome, like peak readiness or a migration, so value shows up in a quarter.

Developers push back

I bring developer experience leads in early and show the golden paths that save them time.

Security review of integrations

I start the security package in discovery, with read only scopes mapped for every integration.

No one owns engineering operations

I find the leader who runs the OpEx review and make them the champion.

Budget and value sit in different orgs

The business case shows value for both, and the close is timed to the planning cycle.

MEDDPICC checklist

Updated after every call and reviewed with my manager each week. Select a letter to flip it to the proof I need before the deal counts in my forecast.

Commit ruleA deal enters commit only when the economic buyer has confirmed the metrics, the evaluation has met written criteria, the paper process and partner steps are dated in the mutual plan, and the champion has been tested.
