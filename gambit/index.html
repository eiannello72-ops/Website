What I know about Gambit

The product, the buyer, and why this year matters.

The product

Balens is an AI native cyber resilience posture management platform. It maps applications and their dependencies across cloud, backups, IaC and security tools, scores resilience, validates recovery paths and produces audit ready evidence.2

It is vendor agnostic, integrates with Wiz and is sold on AWS Marketplace.2,6

The buyer

The CISO signs, with the CIO as co owner because recovery runs through infrastructure. Champions sit in cloud platform, DevOps, SecDevOps and backup teams, the people who know where recovery would break.7

Wiz and AWS account teams are natural allies, since Gambit adds recovery context to tools the customer already runs.

The moment

Founders Alon Gromakov, Sa'ar Elias and May Kogan are Unit 8200 veterans and early Sentra employees. They raised $61M in under 12 months.1,8

Gambit's research team published the findings on the AI assisted breach of nine Mexican government agencies and the LA Metro attack, which gives a new brand a reason for a CISO to take the meeting.5,9

The market Gambit is creating

AI made attacks cheap

One operator using two AI platforms breached nine Mexican government agencies, and agents compromised online retailers for about $25 each. Attacks will get through, so the board question becomes how fast the business comes back.4,9

Regulators want proof

NYDFS Part 500 requires covered companies to test restoring critical systems from backups at least once a year. Gromakov's argument is that resilience should be validated continuously, since the estate changes daily.8,10

Backups are one layer

Each backup vendor reports on the data it protects. An application also needs identity, networking, IaC and other vendors' copies to come back, and that full map goes stale the day it ships. Balens keeps it current, vendor agnostic.11

How deals are won against each alternative

Where each option is strong, where Gambit wins, and the question I would ask in discovery. Select an alternative to open its battlecard.

Backup platforms

Most common

Where they are strong

They hold the data copies, offer immutability and recovery tooling, and own the budget and the relationship with infrastructure teams.

Where Gambit wins

Vendor agnostic across every backup tool in the estate, and it checks the whole application, including identity, networking and IaC, which sit outside any one backup vendor's view.

The question I ask

When one application depends on two backup vendors and Terraform, who tells you it will come back?

Cloud native DR

Entrenched

Where they are strong

Already in the account, priced low and easy for platform teams to switch on.

Where Gambit wins

Built for an adversary as well as an outage. Ransomware operators can sit in an environment for weeks and target recovery first, and Balens covers every cloud and vendor in one view.3

The question I ask

If the attacker has been in the account for weeks, how do you know the DR copy comes back clean?

Drift and IaC tools

Adjacent

Where they are strong

Cloud asset inventory, codifying unmanaged resources and catching drift from Terraform.

Where Gambit wins

Ties drift to the business application and its recovery target, alongside backups and security context.

The question I ask

When drift is found, how do you know which business service it puts at risk?

CNAPP and posture

Partner first

Where they are strong

Deep visibility into exposure, identities and data across the cloud, with strong executive relationships.

Where Gambit wins

Gambit answers a different question, whether the business can recover, and it already attaches Wiz issues to recovery gaps.6

The question I ask

Your CNAPP shows what is exposed. What shows you what comes back?

Status quo

Always present

Where they are strong

Familiar, already budgeted, and enough to pass last year's audit.

Where Gambit wins

Continuous evidence across every application, with gaps found before an incident.

The question I ask

What has changed in your environment since the last test?

Where these deals stall, and what I do about it

Security and infrastructure split ownership

I bring the CISO and CIO into one success plan early, with recovery time as a shared metric.

"Our backup vendor already does this"

I map one application end to end, showing the identity, network, IaC and second vendor its recovery depends on.

"We passed our DR test"

I ask what has changed since the test, then show the drift on a single service.

Access to production takes time

I bring cloud security and architecture in during discovery and scope the evaluation to a few services first.

No budget line for resilience

The business case prices downtime, audit hours and insurance terms, and AWS Marketplace lets them use existing commit.

A new category with few references

I lead with Gambit's research, give executives access to the founders, and write the evaluation criteria together.
