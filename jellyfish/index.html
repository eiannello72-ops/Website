What I know about Jellyfish

The product, the buyer, and why this year matters.

The product

Jellyfish turns data from Git, Jira and AI coding tools into one view of engineering, across AI Impact, delivery, allocations, developer experience and DevFinOps.5

Its edge is the dataset: 100 million pull requests, 90 million prompts and 150 trillion tokens analyzed, which feeds benchmarks no customer could build alone.2

The buyer

The CTO or SVP of Engineering signs. The CFO joins when the case includes AI spend or software capitalization. Champions sit in engineering operations, platform and developer productivity teams.1

AI tool vendors are natural partners. Jellyfish already measures Copilot, Cursor, Amazon Q Developer, Kiro and Augment in production.6,7

The moment

AI coding spend has moved from pilots to budget lines, and Jellyfish data shows the top 10% of developers now spend about $200 a week.8

Jellyfish has led G2's software development analytics grid for 16 straight quarters and is coming off a year of record growth.1,9

The market Jellyfish is defining

AI spend needs a return

More tokens buy more output at a steep price. Jellyfish found the top 10% of engineers produce about twice the median while spending ten times the tokens.4 Every CTO now needs to show which spend pays off.

The category is consolidating

Atlassian agreed to buy DX for about $1 billion in 2025, its largest acquisition.10 Jellyfish stays independent, with data across Jira, Git providers and every major AI coding tool.

Finance has a seat

Where software capitalization and R&D tax reporting run on timesheets, DevFinOps replaces them with engineering activity data and saves finance leaders about 700 hours a year.5

How deals are won against each alternative

Where each option is strong, where Jellyfish wins, and the question I would ask in discovery. Select an alternative to open its battlecard.

DX

Most common

Where they are strong

Developer experience surveys, a well known measurement framework, and the reach of Atlassian's Jira relationships.10

Where Jellyfish wins

Independent of any one toolchain, built on system data from the largest production dataset, with AI spend and DevFinOps for finance.

The question I ask

If you change any part of your toolchain, does your measurement still hold?

Entrenched

Where they are strong

Developer facing workflow automation for pull requests, and fast adoption by team leads.

Adjacent

Where these deals stall, and what I do about it

Engineers worry about surveillance

I bring engineering managers in early, show how leaders use it to fix workflow and systems, and use DevEx surveys to give engineers a voice.

"We built dashboards in Looker"

I price the analysts who maintain them, then show benchmarks and AI tool data an internal build can't reach.

"Our AI vendor gives us usage data"

Each vendor reports on its own tool. I show one view across all of them, tied to delivery.

Security review of Git and Jira access

I start the security package in discovery and bring the CISO's team into the POC plan.

A POC with no finish line

Success criteria, a read out date and the economic buyer are agreed before any data connects.

Finance arrives late

I bring the CFO in with AI spend and capitalization, so the case covers both budgets.

SPICED checklist

Updated after every call and reviewed with my manager each week. Each line needs written proof before the deal counts in my forecast.

S

Situation

What is true in their world today
What I validate

Engineering headcount, AI coding tools in use and who owns the spend, the Git and Jira stack, how engineering reports to the board, and recent changes like a new CTO, a reorg or an AI mandate.

What I ask
  • Walk me through how engineering reports progress to the exec team today.
  • Which AI coding tools are in use, and who pays for them?
Proof I collect

A one page situation summary the champion confirmed in writing after the first call.

Red flag, and my move

"We're just exploring." I send back the situation summary and ask what I got wrong. Corrections tell me what matters.

P

Pain

The problem in their words
What I validate

AI spend with no clear return, roadmaps that slip, status meetings standing in for data, or timesheets for capitalization, felt by a named leader.

What I ask
  • What question from the board can't you answer today?
  • Who feels this most, and what has it cost them?
Proof I collect

The pain stated by at least two leaders, one in engineering and one in finance.

Red flag, and my move

Pain only the champion feels. I take the benchmark data to a second leader and test whether it lands.

I

Impact

What solving it is worth
What I validate

The dollar value of AI spend that ships, engineering capacity returned to the roadmap, faster cycle time and finance hours saved, plus what it means for the CTO personally.

What I ask
  • What would a 20% faster cycle time be worth to the roadmap?
  • If you cut the AI spend that doesn't ship, what would you fund instead?
Proof I collect

Impact in dollars in the business case, checked by finance.

Red flag, and my move

"Productivity" with no number attached. I bring their peer group's benchmarks and ask where they want to land.

C

Critical event

The date that forces a decision
What I validate

Next year's AI budget, an AI tool renewal, a board meeting, fiscal year end or an audit, and what happens if they miss it.

What I ask
  • When do next year's AI budgets lock?
  • What happens on that date if nothing has changed?
Proof I collect

The event dated in the mutual action plan, with the consequence of missing it written down.

Red flag, and my move

A date with no consequence, like "we'd like it by Q1." I tie the close plan to a real event. Without one, the deal stays upside, out of commit.

D

Decision makers

Who says yes, and who sells for us
What I validate

The CTO or SVP of Engineering as economic buyer, the CFO when spend or capitalization is in the case, anyone with a veto, and a champion in engineering operations or platform I have tested by asking them to act.

What I ask
  • If this works, whose number gets better?
  • Who could stop this, even if everyone else says yes?
Proof I collect

A meeting with the economic buyer before the POC, and a champion who got me a meeting I could not get alone.

Red flag, and my move

"I'll take it to the CTO." I write the champion a one page brief and ask for 20 minutes together to pressure test it.

D

Decision criteria

How they judge us, and against whom
What I validate

Coverage of every AI tool, benchmark depth, allocation accuracy, security and finance outputs, ranked by the people who score them, plus every alternative: DX, LinearB, Swarmia, vendor dashboards, an in house build and doing nothing.

What I ask
  • What would make Jellyfish the obvious choice, and what would rule us out?
  • If you don't choose us, what do you do instead?
Proof I collect

Written POC success criteria drafted with the champion, and each alternative named with its internal sponsor.

Red flag, and my move

Criteria copied from a survey tool's feature list. I reshape them early around system data, benchmarks and AI spend, using the battlecard questions.

D

Decision process

Every step to a signature
What I validate

Demo, POC and read out, then security review of Git and Jira access, DPA, legal, procurement thresholds, who signs and fiscal year end.

What I ask
  • Walk me through how you bought your last engineering platform.
  • What did security need from your last engineering data vendor?
Proof I collect

A mutual action plan dated back from the critical event, with the security package started in discovery.

Red flag, and my move

Nobody can say what happens after the POC. I map the post POC steps with the champion before the POC begins, and meet procurement once the business case is agreed.

Commit ruleA deal enters commit only when the economic buyer has confirmed the impact, the critical event is dated, the decision process is mapped through signature in the mutual plan, and the POC read out is done.
