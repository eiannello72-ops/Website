Where these deals stall, and what I do about it

"We'll script it ourselves"

I price the engineer hours to build and maintain it, then show a working story in an afternoon.

Security and IT buy separately

I find the workflow both teams share, like offboarding or phishing, and bring both leaders into one plan.

An evaluation with no finish line

Success criteria, the use cases and a read out date are agreed before the Solutions Engineer builds anything.

AI makes the CISO nervous

I lead with isolated execution, hidden credentials and full logging, and bring the security review in early.

The SOAR renewal slips past

The incumbent's renewal and notice dates go into the mutual plan in discovery, and the close is timed to them.

The reseller isn't aligned

I bring the partner in at the first meeting, so the paper process and the relationship work for us.

MEDDPICC checklist

Updated after every call and reviewed with my manager each week. Each line needs written proof before the deal counts in my forecast.

M

Metrics

The number the deal is sold on
What I validate

Hours spent on repeat tickets and alert handling, time to build a workflow today, SOAR and script maintenance cost, and the risk of ungoverned AI.

What I ask
  • How many hours a week go into your ten most common tickets or alerts?
  • What does your current SOAR cost, including the engineers who run it?
Proof I collect

Baseline hours and cost in their own numbers, checked by finance and written into the business case.

Red flag, and my move

A problem nobody has priced. I bring the Jamf, Snowflake and OpenTable results and ask them to correct the numbers for their team.

E

Economic buyer

The person who can say yes alone
What I validate

The CIO or CISO, sometimes both, with the CTO when engineering teams build in 3B, and how they made their last platform decision.

What I ask
  • Whose budget pays for automation today?
  • If this works, whose number gets better?
Proof I collect

A meeting with the economic buyer before the evaluation, and their success criteria in their own words.

Red flag, and my move

"I'll take it to the CIO." I write the champion a one page brief and ask for 20 minutes together to pressure test it.

D

Decision criteria

How they will judge us
What I validate

Speed to build, who can build, security of credentials and execution, AI governance, integrations with their stack, and total cost.

What I ask
  • What has to be true for Tines to be the clear choice?
  • What would make your CISO say no?
Proof I collect

Written evaluation criteria the champion and I drafted together, before the build starts.

Red flag, and my move

Criteria copied from a SOAR feature list. I reshape the criteria early around build speed, governance and who can use it.

D

Decision process

Every step to a signature
What I validate

Use case build, security review, business case, legal and procurement, with dates, and how they bought their current automation tools.

What I ask
  • Walk me through how you chose your current SOAR or automation tool.
  • Who could stop this, even if everyone else says yes?
Proof I collect

A mutual action plan dated back from the renewal or budget deadline, edited by both sides.

Red flag, and my move

No one can describe what happens after the evaluation. I map the post evaluation steps with the champion before the build starts.

P

Paper process

How the contract actually gets signed
What I validate

Security questionnaire, DPA, legal redlines, procurement thresholds, the reseller's role, AWS Marketplace options and the incumbent's renewal dates.

What I ask
  • Do you prefer to buy through a reseller or your cloud marketplace?
  • When does the current contract renew, and how much notice does it need?
Proof I collect

Security package sent in week one, partner and paper steps in the plan, and a signature date agreed.

Red flag, and my move

Procurement first hears about us at quarter end. I bring the partner in early and meet procurement as soon as the business case is agreed.

I

Identify pain

Why they act this year
What I validate

A ticket backlog, analyst burnout, a SOAR renewal, an AI incident or an audit finding about ungoverned tools.

What I ask
  • What happens if nothing changes for the next 12 months?
  • What broke last quarter that a workflow would have caught?
Proof I collect

Pain tied to a dollar figure and a date, confirmed by IT and security leaders.

Red flag, and my move

Pain with no cost and no deadline. I go back to the rational drowning step with their own numbers until it has both.

C

Champion

Who sells when I am not in the room
What I validate

A security or IT automation lead who builds, has influence and wins personally if this succeeds. I test it by asking them to act.

What I ask
  • How would you present the evaluation results to your CIO?
  • Could you get us 30 minutes with security operations this week?
Proof I collect

They built a story with us, coached me on the room, and got me a meeting I could not get alone.

Red flag, and my move

A fan of the product with no pull on the decision. I keep them close and multithread to someone who can move the deal.

C

Competition

Every option they could pick
What I validate

Torq, Cortex XSOAR, Splunk SOAR, iPaaS tools, AI SOC point products, in house scripts and doing nothing, plus who inside favors each.

What I ask
  • If you don't choose us, what do you do instead?
  • Who on the team would push for that option?
Proof I collect

Each alternative named with its internal sponsor, and our strengths built into the evaluation criteria.

Red flag, and my move

We learn about the competitor at the proposal. I ask the battlecard questions in discovery, so the criteria reflect where Tines wins.

Commit ruleA deal enters commit only when the economic buyer has confirmed the metrics, the evaluation has met written criteria, the paper process and partner steps are dated in the mutual plan, and the champion has been tested.
